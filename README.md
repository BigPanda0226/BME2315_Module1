# BME2315_Module1
Repository for BME 2315 Module 1 with Matthew and Francesco

Our project investigated whether sex, age at death, and known head injury history were associated with amyloid-beta 42 (Aβ42) concentrations in a donor dataset. We used a Python Patient class to organize the data and select groups for analysis. We compared female and male donors with dementia and donors with and without known head injuries using two-sided, unpaired t tests. We also performed linear regression to examine the relationship between age at death and Aβ42, excluding one donor whose Aβ42 concentration was more than three standard deviations above the mean.

Female donors with dementia had a higher mean Aβ42 concentration than male donors, but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.4577). Donors with known head injuries had a lower mean concentration than those without known injuries, but this difference was also not significant (p = 0.1328). After removing the outlier, age explained only about 1.1% of the variation in Aβ42 (R² = 0.0108). These results did not provide strong evidence of the relationships we investigated. The sample size, variability, and limited injury information restrict our conclusions, and the findings should not guide individual patient care. Future work could examine more donors, account for other factors, and compare the regression results with and without the excluded donor.
